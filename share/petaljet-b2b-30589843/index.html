1 What is actually happening to your gate right now

Everything in this section came from petaljet.com page source, logged out, no admin access. First, credit where it's due — part of your gate still works. The price element on a collection page really is empty for a logged-out visitor:

// petaljet.com/collections/premium-roses — logged out <span class="money" data-price><!-- empty --></span> ← the app is still blanking this

So the symptom you're describing isn't that the price box filled back in. It's that the price was never only in the price box. It's escaping through three other doors, and two of them were always open.

Leak 1 — Shopify's own analytics object, on every page you serve

Every Shopify storefront embeds a meta object for analytics. Yours carries the full price and the full variant name:

// view-source on any collection page, logged out var meta = {"products":[{ "id":1791250432061, "vendor":"F260", "handle":"3d-rose", "variants":[ {"id":52781511803189, "price":9000, "public_title":"Ecuador 50CM - 60 Stems ($1.50 Each)"}, {"id":52781513572661, "price":8976, "public_title":"Ecuador 60CM - 48 Stems ($1.87 Each)"}, {"id":52781513802037, "price":12096, "public_title":"Ecuador 70CM - 72 Stems ($1.68 Each)"} ]}, … ]}
This is injected by Shopify, not by your theme. A price-hiding app cannot remove it — the app runs inside your theme, and this object is written before your theme is. So this door was open the entire time Login To View Prices was working. Nothing about legacy accounts changed it.

Leak 2 — the public products endpoint

Every Shopify store exposes a JSON catalog. I pulled all 863 of your products with live case prices and your farm vendor codes, logged out, in a single request:

curl https://petaljet.com/products.json?limit=250 → 863 products · ~1,640 variants · 27+ farm codes (F214, F231, F260, F269…) → case prices $51.30 – $255.96 (median $116.80) → per-stem $0.59 – $4.63 (median $1.79)

Leak 3 — you typed the price into the product name

This is the one that matters most, and it's the only one you control. Your variant names read:

"3D Rose - Ecuador 50CM - 60 Stems ($1.50 Each)"

In a sample of 250 products, 473 of 474 variants — 99.8% — have the stem count and the per-stem price typed into the variant name. A name is not a price field, so no price-hiding app touches it. It travels everywhere the product travels: search results, JSON, analytics, cart, email.

The honest read: the gate you're asking to get back was never really a gate. It hid the price element. It never hid the price. That's not a criticism of the app — it's the ceiling of what any theme-level app can do. An app painted on top of a D2C store cannot solve this, because the leak is underneath the paint. The only version that holds is one where wholesale pricing isn't on the public product in the first place.

3 Your three requirements on Plus, one by one

Solved

1. Account creation only if approved

Delivered as approval-gated ordering, not blocked signup.

Company account requests: a wholesale application form on your storefront. On submit, Shopify automatically creates the company, the company location and the customer — and drops them into an "Ordering not approved" queue in your admin.

Your custom fields — resale permit number, business license, number of shops — save as metafields on the company profile. To reject, you delete the company. Every account now has a real application behind it.

Solved

2. Automatic approval

A native Shopify Flow template does exactly this: Allow ordering for companies created by company account requests.

Turn it on and every submitted application is approved the moment it lands — instant pricing, no human in the loop. That's the minimal-friction experience you described, and it's faster than the Talon flow ever was.

Better than a blanket rule: branch on your own fields. Resale permit filled in → auto-approve. Blank, or a free email domain → hold for review. Pair it with Send B2B access email to approved customer.

Not possible — and I won't pretend

3. One-step login

This one you don't get back. New customer accounts is email-code only. There is no password option, on any plan.

Sessions do persist, so it's not a code on every visit. And third-party identity providers (OAuth 2.0 / OpenID Connect) are supported if you ever front the store with your own system.

What genuinely improves: each buyer at a shop gets their own login under one company, with their own permissions, instead of a whole florist sharing one set of credentials. That's usually the real pain behind "one-step login."

4 Your two business problems — and what they're costing

Problem 1: consumers placing orders they can't handle

"Everyday consumers are placing large orders, and they do not know how to handle or process our products properly, resulting in requests for refunds and poor reviews."

B2B ends this. Wholesale pricing moves off the public product and into a catalog assigned to approved companies. A consumer who authenticates isn't attached to a company, so a 200-stem case at $1.50 a stem does not exist for them. There is no version of the page where they can buy it.

5 Why the price is in your product names — and why Plus is what unlocks it

This started as a workaround and has quietly become a constraint. With no per-unit pricing field and no volume-break structure, the per-stem figure got typed into the variant name — 1,600-odd times.

Two costs. The first is maintenance — every price change is a text edit on a product name, against daily-changing availability across 27 farms. The second is the one nobody notices until it bites:

The naming scheme only works because there is exactly one price. ($1.50 Each) is printed in the product name, so it's true for every buyer — because every buyer pays it. The day you give your biggest florist a negotiated rate, that name is wrong on their screen, and there is no way to fix it short of duplicating the catalog. Your product data is built on never tiering your pricing.

The migration undoes both at once. Stem count, length and farm move into metafields, and the per-stem figure is computed in the theme — price ÷ stem count — so it's derived instead of typed. The variant name collapses to Ecuador 50CM. Pricing then lives where it belongs: in catalogs, with quantity rules and volume breaks underneath, and a different catalog per company where you've negotiated one.

Shopify's native unit pricing won't do this — I checked. It only supports measurement units (weight, volume, length); there's no "per stem." Metafields plus a line of theme logic is the route, and it's part of the build.

7 What else comes with Plus

B2B is the reason you'd move. These are the things you get in the same box — and two of them go straight at problems you're already living with.

A checkout you actually control

Right now your checkout is Shopify's default, and you take what it gives you. On Plus it becomes yours to build on. Shopify's documentation is explicit that checkout UI extensions and checkout branding require Shopify Plus — this is the part of the platform that is genuinely gated.

For a perishable business shipping from 27 farms, that's not a cosmetic upgrade:

Ask the questions you need answered

  • Delivery-date picker — the single most important field on a flower order, and today it isn't there
  • Event date vs. ship date, so a wedding order can't arrive four days early
  • PO number and buyer reference on B2B orders
  • Farm / ship-week selection surfaced at checkout instead of in an email thread afterward

Enforce the rules instead of emailing about them

  • Order-cutoff validation — block a Thursday order for Friday delivery before it's placed, not after
  • Case-minimum and increment rules carried through to checkout
  • Hide or rename shipping and payment options by buyer, region or order value
  • Hydration solution and care add-ons offered in-checkout on the order they belong to

And because B2B and D2C run as separate flows in one store, your florists can get terms, PO fields and a case-minimum checkout while a retail buyer never sees any of it. Branding is API-controlled, so checkout stops looking like a generic Shopify page and starts looking like Petaljet.

Shopify Audiences — and you already qualify on all three counts

Audiences is Plus-exclusive, requires Shopify Payments, and is US/Canada only. You're US-based and you've been on Shopify Payments since 2019 — the only box left is the plan. It's free to install; you pay your normal ad spend and nothing to Shopify.
