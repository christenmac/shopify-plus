10 Automation — Shopify Flow

Overview ↗

Shopify Flow is Shopify's native automation platform — a no-code trigger → condition → action builder that eliminates repetitive ops work and scales your team without adding headcount. Plus gets more extensive access to Flow than any other plan — the highest usage limits, custom partner-app tasks, and the ability to automate Plus-exclusive surfaces like B2B, multi-store, and Launchpad.

How It Works
  • Trigger — an event occurs (e.g. an order is placed)
  • Condition — criteria are checked (e.g. order value > $500)
  • Action — automated response fires (e.g. tag customer as VIP, notify team)
What You Can Automate
  • Order Management — flag high-risk orders, cancel restricted-region orders, reward high-value customers with VIP status
  • Inventory Control — trigger supplier reorders when stock drops, hide low-stock products, pause marketing campaigns automatically
  • Customer Loyalty — build tiered VIP programs ($300+ → Premium VIP, $100–$299 → Silver VIP) with personalized follow-ups
  • Product Organization — auto-tag products by description, dynamically populate collections as new inventory arrives
  • B2B & Wholesale — notify sales teams of new wholesale orders, automate wholesale customer tiering
Top 5 Workflows Plus Merchants Actually Run
  • 1 · High-value order → VIP tagging
    Order created → order total is over your VIP threshold → tag the customer VIP and add them to a segment. Drives tiered loyalty (e.g. $300+ → Premium, $100–299 → Silver) and lets Klaviyo or Audiences target the tag automatically. Usually the first workflow a merchant builds.
  • 2 · Fraud & risk hold on checkout
    Order risk analyzed → risk is Medium or High → hold fulfillment, tag the order, and email or Slack the ops team before anything ships. If you capture manually, only auto-capture when risk is Low. Replaces manual review of every order and stops chargebacks at the door.
  • 3 · Low-stock reorder alert
    Inventory quantity changed → variant drops to your reorder point → email the vendor a reorder request, Slack the buying team, and tag the product low-stock. Build note: gate on both the current and prior quantity, or the email refires on every subsequent order until restock.
  • 4 · Publish / unpublish on stock status
    Inventory quantity changed → hits zero → unpublish the product (or hide it from the collection) and pause the ads pointing at it; republish and notify marketing on restock. Kills the "sold out" bounce and the spend behind it.
  • 5 · B2B / wholesale order routing
    Order created → the order is attached to a B2B company or a wholesale-tagged customer → notify the assigned rep, apply the company's payment terms, tag for the wholesale fulfillment queue, and tier the company on cumulative spend.

Plus-Exclusive Delivery Controls Plus Exclusive

Custom Shipping Discounts ↗
  • Custom shipping discounts in Checkout Blocks Plus Only — four discount types you can create:
    • Percentage shipping discounts — lower qualifying shipping options by a set percent
    • Free shipping — a 100% discount on qualifying shipping options
    • Fixed amount shipping discounts — subtract a set amount; if it exceeds the delivery cost, shipping is free
    • Fixed price shipping offers — bring qualifying options down to a price you choose, applied only when the original rate is higher
  • Targeted by market, cart subtotal or quantity, B2B status, company, customer tag, order count, collection, SKU, or shipping country / state / ZIP
  • Automatic or code-based, combinable with product and order discounts, capped by maximum uses, and scheduled with start & end dates — so a free-shipping threshold can run a campaign window and switch itself off
  • Delivery customization Functions — hide express shipping for PO boxes, reorder options so the profitable method sits first, or rename a method to something buyers understand ("Free 2-Day — Members")
  • Cart & checkout validation Functions — block restricted-region or over-quantity orders before payment rather than cancelling and refunding after (see §9)
