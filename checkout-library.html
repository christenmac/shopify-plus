On the payment step: a link can't jump straight to it. Shopify only renders payment once contact details, a shipping address and a shipping rate exist, and a URL can't supply those — so the deepest a public link reaches is the checkout's first step. That is where every in-checkout extension lives, and the test address above gets you to payment in about ten seconds. If you need a payment step that is customized on demand, use the checkout-preview demo instead — this library is for showing real merchants' real checkouts.

How each entry was verified

The link works. For every store we read /products.json, picked an available, non-gift-card variant, built https://<domain>/cart/<variant>:1, followed it with a cookie jar, and kept the entry only if the browser ended up on a live /checkouts/… URL. 218 domains were tested; 120 passed and are listed above. The rest were removed rather than badged.

What the elements mean. Evidence is the app extensions and scripts a store actually loads, plus which /policies/* pages it publishes, plus real bundle/kit SKUs in its catalogue. That tells you what the merchant runs. It does not prove where they place it — app presence is a strong hint, not a guarantee of a checkout block. Click through and confirm before you screenshare; the tick box on each row is there to record that.

Known blind spot. Headless and custom storefronts (Gymshark, SKIMS) don't expose their app scripts this way, so they score low here while often running rich checkouts. Low coverage means "we couldn't see it", not "they don't have it".

Freshness. Variant IDs go out of stock. Re-run the probe if a link starts landing on a homepage or cart page — that is the signature of a dead variant.
